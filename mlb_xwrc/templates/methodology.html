01 / THE THREE NUMBERS

Actual. Expected. Projected.

wRC+ measures offensive production relative to league average, with park and league adjustments. An index of 100 is average; 120 represents production 20% above average.

{{ s.year }} xwRC+ is this project’s XGBoost estimate based on contact quality and plate discipline. It is a model estimate, not an official FanGraphs or Statcast metric. Its inputs include walk and strikeout rates, barrels, exit velocity, launch angle, and available discipline measures.

The {{ s.year+1 }} forecast combines chronological expected performance, actual wRC+, and a three-year PA/recency-weighted history, then applies a smoothed and shrunken conditional aging adjustment.

02 / THE SIGN CONVENTION

Read the gap in the right direction.

Gap = actual − expected. A negative number means the hitter’s observed result was below the model’s estimate; a positive number means it was above. Forecast change is next season’s projection minus this season’s actual result.

Candidate tabs use a {{ s.config.get('gap_threshold',10) }}-point screen. This is a research heuristic, not a significance test. The default player view requires {{ s.config.min_rank_pa }} PA and no more than 40% missing selected features. Lowering the PA filter broadens the sample and increases uncertainty.

03 / HONEST LIMITS

There is more to a player than a point estimate.

Discrepancies can reflect randomness, omitted skills, park effects, measurement error, and model bias. A large gap does not establish that a hitter was lucky, unlucky, or certain to regress.

The 90% empirical ranges come from earlier out-of-time forecast errors. They are conditional on returning hitters who meet the PA threshold, and they do not guarantee future coverage. Injuries, playing time, retirement, and changes in the league environment are outside the model.

Aging effects are observational and affected by survivor selection. Sparse ages shrink toward zero. The final holdout’s aging contribution was {{ s.age_gain|num(2) }} MAE points; the overall forecasting improvement should not be credited entirely to age.
